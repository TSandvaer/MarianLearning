### 0.2 What changed from the draft, and why

1. **`thumb` → out.** The draft carried `thumb` as the body-part anchor with its silent `b` flagged as "the pool's #1 uncertainty." Dave ruled it out of the opening 7: the silent `b` "violates the one-new-element-per-session principle" when stacked on a new digraph (Dave §3e). Dave's Recommendation 5 + Non-obvious finding 4 both name `thumb` as **the** canonical pool-EXTENSION word — strongest candidate to add *after* the 7-word arc, not in it. Net: `thumb` moves from the pool to the documented extension slot (§7 Q3).
2. **`cloth` → in, as `hybridMode: true`.** Dave's recommended 7 includes `cloth` (short-o, th-final, picturable square of fabric). Its `/kl/` onset is a consonant blend beyond CVC scope, so it ships recognition-only — `hybridMode: true`, planner emits no decode/spell prompts (Dave §3d "The cloth problem resolved", §3e). `cloth` replaces `thumb` as the pool's 7th word and gives the pool a second short-o word-final entry alongside `moth`.
3. **`thick` → reclassified `hybridMode: true`.** The draft treated `thick`'s `ck` coda as "acoustically inert" and kept it `hybridMode: false`. Dave's analysis (§3d "Thick reconsidered", §3e) is that `thick` requires TWO digraphs — `th` (this tier's target) AND `ck` (a separate, not-yet-formally-taught digraph) — making it a double-digraph word. In the recognition format the `ck` is not a decoding burden, but `hybridMode: true` is the correct flag: planner must never ask Marian to decode or spell `thick`. Net: the th tier ships **2** `hybridMode` words (`thick`, `cloth`), not 0.
4. **`hybridMode` count: 0 → 2.** The draft mirrored the ch tier (0 hybridMode). Reconciled, the th tier resembles the **sh** tier's structure: 5 fully decodable + 2 hybridMode. The reason differs from sh's (sh's hybrids were long-vowel-after-digraph; th's are onset-blend + double-digraph — Dave Non-obvious finding 5), but the planner guard logic is identical and already exists from the sh tier (PR #220) — no new schema work.
5. **Word-final th confirmed for the FIRST tier.** The draft flagged "pending Dave: first tier or follow-up arc?" Dave confirms word-final th is not only in-scope for the first tier but is the *stronger* stock — 5 of 7 final is "intentional and correct, not a gap" (Dave §3f, Non-obvious finding 1). The introduction *order* is still initial-before-final (`thin, thick` first), but the pool *weighting* is final-heavy by design.
6. **Mouth-position visual is now mandatory.** The draft had this as Thomas's open A/B/C choice with Option B (static illustration) recommended. Dave's addendum elevates it to a non-negotiable design requirement: "mouth-at-teeth visual is required ... this is not optional" (Dave Recommendation 4). Option A (script-only, no visual) is therefore off the table. §7 Q1 is narrowed to Option B vs Option C (static vs animated) — both honour Dave; the choice is cost/richness only.
7. **`think`/`thank` dropped from reserve.** The draft held `think`/`thank` as §7 Q3 reserve fill. Dave §4e explicitly rejects them as unpicturable. They are removed from the reserve list; `thumb` is the documented pool-extension word instead.
8. **`/θ/`-vs-`/s/` second trap class: NOT shipped.** The draft flagged this §7 Q2 pending Dave. Dave's addendum names th-vs-t as the singular primary distractor concern and does not raise /s/. Confirmed: th/t contrast only in v1.

## Why this spec, why now

Per Dave's research (`digraph-acquisition-marian.md` §Q1/§Q2 + §Q3, and pending the th-addendum):

- The digraph introduction order is **`sh` first, `ch` second, voiceless `th` /θ/ third.** Voiced `/ð/` is deferred entirely (sight-word domain — parent note §Q2 4th row, §Q3 "th voiced", Bottom-line).
- `sh` shipped 2026-05-14 (`digraphs-sh` tier — 7-word pool, planner directive, committed canon, regression spec). `ch` is mid-build (PR #225 spec + #227 wordPack; planner/canon in flight). `th` (voiceless /θ/) is the next tier in the locked sequence.
- **Voiceless /θ/ third is a deliberate pedagogical choice, not just an ordering** (parent note §Q2 Sources 7–9, Bottom-line): voiceless /θ/ "requires the most explicit articulation scaffolding (no L1 reference) and the picturable content-word stock is smaller than sh or ch." It is introduced after sh and ch are consolidated so the conceptual load of the new articulation is manageable — "two letters, one sound" is by now thoroughly reviewed, not new.
- **/θ/ is the HARDEST of the three digraphs for Marian** (parent note §Q3, Sources 10–12): Tagalog has no /θ/ and no /ð/. The substitution `/θ/ → /t/` ("think" → "tink", "thank" → "tank") is "the most systematic of the three" and "structural, not a beginner error that resolves with exposure." Crucially — **this is a PRODUCTION error, not a perception error.** Parent note §Q3 is explicit: "In the chip-tap format, the production error is not directly testable — what matters is whether Marian can match the spoken word (Emma says 'thin') to the text chip 'thin' vs. 'tin' or 'sin.' The spelling difference makes this visually unambiguous as long as the distractor words don't also start with th." The chip-tap recognition task is achievable; the /θ/→/t/ substitution does not block it.
- **The ONE thing /θ/ has that sh and ch did not: it needs an articulation-placement scaffold** (parent note §Q3): "Emma's instruction note: 'th together says a special sound — put your tongue between your teeth and blow.' A brief animation of Emma's mouth position (or an illustration of a mouth) would help more than TTS alone here." This is the th-tier analogue of the ch tier's c-says-/k/ script clause — see §4.
- **Voiced `/ð/` must be EXCLUDED from this tier entirely** (parent note Bottom-line, §Q3): `the, this, that, they, them, then, than, there` are voiced /ð/ function words — low-imageability, the /ð/→/d/ substitution is "deeply embedded in Philippine English at all levels", and they "share a grapheme with the voiceless form Marian will have just learned, creating a two-realizations-of-one-grapheme puzzle that exceeds the scope of first digraph introduction." If they ever appear, they arrive as **sight words** (Emma reads, Marian matches text), NOT as digraph phonics items. **No voiced-th word is a th-tier target or distractor.** See §1.4.
- 8–10 words per digraph; the parent note §Q4 notes the th picturable content-word stock "is smaller than sh or ch" — the th tier ships at **7** (matching the sh + ch tier depth). Dave's th-addendum §3f confirms 7 is the defensible pool, with `thumb` as the documented pool-extension word if 7 proves thin (Dave Recommendation 5).
- **Vocabulary-familiarity is the binding constraint** — same as sh and ch (parent note §Q4). Picture-grounding the chip is the design response. The drafted th pool is chosen for picture-stability; §3 carries the per-word picture concerns.

**Scope of this spec:** word selection (RECONCILED against Dave's th-addendum — §0, §1), focus-node naming guidance (not architecture — the `digraphs-th-voiceless` `SkillNode` already exists per Kevin's PR #211 3-sibling split), picture-pack requirements summary, the `phoneme: '/θ/'` schema posture and **why th SETS `phoneme` where ch omitted it** (§6.1 — the load-bearing schema divergence from ch), the `hybridMode` posture (§6.2 — 2 of 7: `thick`, `cloth`), and the open questions for Thomas (§7).

**Out-of-scope (deferred):**

- `SkillNode` shape — already locked in PR #211 (`digraphs-sh`, `digraphs-ch`, `digraphs-th-voiceless` — 3 sibling nodes). This spec consumes the `digraphs-th-voiceless` node.
- **Voiced `/ð/` content** — deferred indefinitely from chip-tap decoding (sight-word domain, parent note Bottom-line / §Q3). `the/this/that/they` are a future sight-word-tier concern, NOT this spec's. See §1.4.
- **wh / ck digraphs** — parent note §Q2 "On wh and ck": out of scope; they follow th in the skill tree as separate specs.
- **sh/ch/th three-way interleaving rules** — parent note §Q5 + Bottom-line: interleave sh and ch once both reach ~70%; th joins the interleave only after IT is consolidated. Cross-pool distractors (`thin` vs `shin` vs `chin`) belong to the _interleaving_ phase, NOT the th-introduction tier (§2 below). A planner-side session-composition decision for a future interleaving spec.
- Distractor architecture refactor — the sh tier built the cross-orthography distractor pattern; ch reused it; th reuses it again. §2 / §6 specify the th-specific rows, no new architecture.

## 1.4 Voiced /ð/ exclusion — the load-bearing call for the voiceless-th tier

This section is the th-tier analogue of the ch tier's §1.3 (the long-vowel allowance) — the one design call that most defines the tier's scope.

**No voiced /ð/ word is a th-tier target OR distractor.** The excluded set: `the, this, that, they, them, then, than, there` (and `with`, which is /ð/ in some dialects — voicing-ambiguous, a conservative exclude).

Rationale (parent research note Bottom-line, §Q2 4th row, §Q3 "th voiced"):

> Defer voiced /ð/ indefinitely in the chip-tap decoding format — it dominates function words (the, this, that, they) that are more productively treated as sight words and that share a grapheme with the voiceless form Marian will have just learned, creating a two-realizations-of-one-grapheme puzzle that exceeds the scope of first digraph introduction.

Three compounding reasons:

1. **Low imageability.** `the/this/that/they` are function words — they cannot be depicted as a standalone chip picture. The chip-tap format is picture-grounded; a function word has no picture.
2. **The /ð/→/d/ substitution is unfixable in this format.** Parent note §Q3 / Sources 12, 13: the /ð/→/d/ substitution "is deeply embedded in Philippine English at all levels" — "structural, not a beginner error." Correcting it "requires sustained phonetic training that is beyond this app's scope."
3. **The same-grapheme puzzle.** `th` spells BOTH /θ/ and /ð/. Introducing both in the same tier "would pit the voiceless /θ/ (content words, picturable, phonics-teachable) against voiced /ð/ (function words, low-imageability, not easily drillable)" — a two-realizations-of-one-grapheme puzzle that exceeds first-digraph-introduction scope.

**Design response:** if `the/this/that/they` ever enter the app, they arrive as **sight words** (Emma reads them aloud correctly; Marian matches text to spoken word without needing to produce /ð/) — a future sight-word-tier concern, NOT this spec. The `digraphs-th-voiceless` SkillNode name (locked in PR #211) already encodes this: the node is explicitly `-voiceless`. A future `digraphs-th-voiced` node, if ever created, is a separate sibling — and per the parent research note it should probably be a sight-word treatment, not a digraph-phonics tier at all.

**Schema consequence — this is why th SETS `phoneme: '/θ/'` (§6.1):** because `th` is the canonical multi-phoneme grapheme, the `WordEntry.phoneme` field exists precisely to scope distractor selection so a future voiced-/ð/ word can never co-occur in a chip trio with a voiceless-/θ/ word. The ch tier omitted `phoneme` (no pack-resident word shares `ch` with a different phoneme); the th tier MUST set it, because the voiced/voiceless split on `th` is the exact case the field was designed for. See §6.1.

### 6.1 `phoneme` annotation — th SETS `phoneme: '/θ/'` on all 7 (the load-bearing schema divergence from ch)

**This is the th tier's most important schema decision, and it diverges from the ch tier.** The ch tier explicitly OMITTED the `phoneme` field (ch-word-list §6 + the wordPack.ts ch-tier comment block: "NO `phoneme` field ... the ch pool needs no such scoping (no pack-resident word shares the `ch` grapheme with a different phoneme)"). **The th tier MUST set `phoneme: '/θ/'` on all 7 targets** — because `th` IS the canonical multi-phoneme grapheme.

The `WordEntry.phoneme` field's own doc comment names this exact case:

> Optional phoneme tag (IPA, content phoneme only). Used for distractor-selection scoping when a grapheme covers multiple phonemes (**e.g. `th` → /θ/ vs /ð/**, `g` → /g/ vs /dʒ/).

Why this matters — three load-bearing reasons:

1. **`th` spells two phonemes.** Voiceless /θ/ (`thin, bath`) and voiced /ð/ (`the, this`). The whole point of the `phoneme` field is to scope distractor selection so a `/θ/` target can never co-occur in a chip trio with a `/ð/` word. Even though §1.4 EXCLUDES all voiced-/ð/ words from v1, a future voiced-th sight-word tier (or any future pack addition) could introduce a `/ð/`-tagged word — and the `phoneme: '/θ/'` tag on the th-tier targets is the structural guarantee that the phoneme-scoping branch in `pickDistractors` will keep them apart. **Setting `phoneme: '/θ/'` now is the architectural floor; omitting it (the ch posture) would leave the door open for a future cross-phoneme co-occurrence bug.** This is exactly the reasoning the sh tier used to set `phoneme: '/ʃ/'`.
2. **It mirrors the sh tier, not the ch tier.** The sh tier set `phoneme: '/ʃ/'` on all 7 sh entries (wordPack.ts sh-tier comment: "All 7 carry `phoneme: '/ʃ/'` — the canonical disambiguator"). The ch tier omitted `phoneme` because `ch` is unambiguously /tʃ/ in this pack — there is no /k/-via-`ch` or /ʃ/-via-`ch` pack-resident word. **th is like sh (multi-phoneme grapheme), NOT like ch (single-phoneme-in-this-pack grapheme).** The `digraphs-th-voiceless` SkillNode name itself encodes the multi-phoneme reality: the node is `-voiceless` precisely because a `-voiced` sibling is conceivable.
3. **The phoneme-scoping branch only rejects a pairing when BOTH target and distractor are phoneme-tagged with DIFFERENT values.** All th-tier distractors are either th-pool neighbours (all `/θ/`-tagged — match, no rejection) or the t-contrast traps `tin`/`tick`/`pat`/`bat`/`mat` (all UNTAGGED — the branch does not fire, by design). So setting `phoneme: '/θ/'` on the targets does NOT break any §2 matrix pairing — it is inert for every v1 th-tier pairing and only becomes active if a future `/ð/`-tagged word is added. **Verified against the sh tier's identical posture: `ship` is `/ʃ/`-tagged, `sip` is untagged, and the `ship`/`sip` pairing passes the check** (wordPack.ts sh-tier comment, lines ~606-608).
