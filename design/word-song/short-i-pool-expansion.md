Short-u shipped 2026-05-09 (PR #170 picture pack, PR #174 canon + planner + debug seed). The pool was deliberately specced ahead of Marian reaching the tier — same mode `short-u-pool-expansion.md` shipped under: design surface lands BEFORE Marian saturates the prior tier, so Kevin's planner-widen and Devon's `wordPack.ts` work can land without blocking on Marian's session pace.

Per Dave's developmental review §6 P0 ("the graduation mechanism to the next vowel must be designed and ticketed _now_, before Marian has repeated sessions on a pool she can navigate from memory rather than decoding") and per `design/research/phonics-sequence-marian.md` §Q1 (vowel order locked at `o → u → i → e`), short-i is the next vowel tier after short-u.

Short-i is **the highest-stakes vowel transition in the entire sequence.** Per Marian's diagnostic (CLAUDE.md "Short vowels: ... teach o → u → e → i" — note the original CLAUDE.md ordering mis-stated; Dave's revised order is `o → u → i → e` per `phonics-sequence-marian.md` §Q1, and this spec lands short-i in the third slot per the corrected order), `/ɪ/` was Marian's weakest vowel at diagnostic time:

> "Short vowels: Short *a* mastered; teach o → u → e → i" (CLAUDE.md)
> "i + s failed", "/i/ sound hard for her" (`project_diagnostic_results` memory)

This is exactly the Category-Goodness assimilation pattern Dave's `short-u-minimal-pair-and-future-vowel-openers.md` §3.1 documents: Tagalog has /i/ (high front tense) but English short-i is /ɪ/ (high front lax) — a different category Marian must build, not a sound she can transfer from her L1. Without explicit contrast scaffolding at first encounter, she is predicted to assimilate /ɪ/ to her existing /i/ and substitute long /iː/ for short /ɪ/ (e.g. reading "sit" as "seat").

**The minimal-pair opener at first encounter is therefore load-bearing for this tier specifically — more so than for short-u.** Dave's research [STRONG] supports the contrast line; the canonical pair is `sit / seat` per his §3.1 recommendation.

The picture pack is a one-time deal of ~50 images across remaining vowel tiers; he prefers batch over re-engaging an agent each time. He needs a curated word list before he can start.

**Scope of this spec:** word selection (audit + final pool), focus-node naming, picture-pack requirements, mastery progression flow into short-i (with the load-bearing `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` opener), canon-bake plan, visual-design delta. The companion MJ prompt sheet [`short-i-picture-pack-prompts.md`](./short-i-picture-pack-prompts.md) carries the per-word generation prompts (Phase 1 deliverable for Thomas).

## 1. Word selection — the 11 short-i words

### Brief / phonics-doc starting pool

The brief and `design/research/phonics-sequence-marian.md` §Application both list a 10-word tentative short-i pool: `pig, sit, hit, lip, tin, bin, wig, dip, fin, kit`. The brief also surfaces Big City Readers (source 5 in the phonics doc) candidates: `sit, hit, big, pig, dig, fish, ship, kid, lip, pin, win, mix, six, fix`.

The brief asks for **~11 target words** (matching short-u's pool size), per the short-u precedent that 8 words saturate too quickly (Marian works through the `cvc-words-short-o` 8-word pool in ~3 sessions before familiarity navigation starts substituting for decoding) and 14 words is short-a's headroom that doesn't replicate at this vowel.

The plan: audit Big City Readers' candidates against v1 word-pack constraints (concrete-noun referent, true CVC pattern, picturable for an L2 8-year-old, vocabulary-cap aware per CLAUDE.md, distinct silhouette at 96pt, no digraph load before the digraphs tier) and reject candidates that fail any constraint.

## 4. Mastery progression flow — graduation through the vowel ladder

This is the canonical hand-off from `cvc-words-short-u` to `cvc-words-short-i`. Mirrors the short-u spec §4 — the mastery rule does the work, the picker just reads `skillLevels`. **PLUS the load-bearing `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` minimal-pair opener at first encounter** (per Dave's `short-u-minimal-pair-and-future-vowel-openers.md` §3.1).

### Stage definitions

```
Session N    : cvc-words-short-u — canonical 11-word pool
Session N+1  : cvc-words-short-i — first short-i session (intro, with /i/ vs. /ɪ/ contrast opener)
```

**No graduation probe at the short-u → short-i boundary**, mirroring the short-u spec §4 "Why first short-u session is not gated on probe." The graduation probe verified generalization within short-a; once that check has fired, every subsequent vowel-tier transition is just standard intro→practicing→mastered through `applyMasteryRule`.

This means short-i inherits the short-u flow:

- Picker walks past `cvc-words-short-u` (now `mastered`) and lands on `cvc-words-short-i` (now `intro`, post-promotion).
- Emma's session-open chatter introduces the new vowel via the **Dave-pre-spec'd `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` contrast line** (next subsection).
- After the first short-i session, the node moves to `practicing` per the standard intro→practicing rule (`mastery.ts`).

### Picker / planner sequencing

`pickFocusNode` does not change for short-i. It walks `WORD_SONG_NODES_IN_ORDER` and stops at the first non-`mastered` node. Adding `cvc-words-short-i` between `cvc-words-short-u` and `digraphs` automatically routes Marian to short-i once short-u is mastered.

The planner gets one new branch in `WORD_SONG_TRACK_GUIDE`: when `focusNode === 'cvc-words-short-i'`, emit `"Read the <word>."` problems drawn from the recommended 11-word short-i pool (or the Phase-2-trimmed pool). `api/_plannerWordList.ts` adds `WORD_SONG_TARGET_WORDS_SHORT_I` matching the final pool.

### What is NOT in this flow

- No "cool-down" gap between `cvc-words-short-u` mastery and `cvc-words-short-i` start. The next session after promotion is the first short-i session.
- No mixed-vowel sessions. `cvc-words-short-i` is short-i-only in v1. Cross-vowel mixing is ticket `86c9m3aek` and stays out of v1 (§9).
- No re-test of short-a, short-o, or short-u inside short-i sessions. The Leitner spaced-review system handles maintenance load (out of scope here).
- No second graduation probe at the short-u → short-i boundary. The `cvc-words` graduation probe was the dual-gate verification needed before exiting short-a; subsequent vowel-tier transitions are standard mastery-rule.
- **No PER-WORD first-encounter scaffolding for individual short-i target words.** Short-o introduced one-time scaffolding for `box`/`fox` because of the `x = /ks/` decoding load. Short-u had no analogous decoding-load entry; short-i ALSO has no analogous entry — every word in the recommended 11-word pool is a clean C-V-C with familiar graphemes. The session-1 `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` minimal-pair opener (next subsection) IS in scope; per-word first-encounter lines are not.

### First-encounter `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` minimal-pair scaffolding (LOAD-BEARING — Dave [STRONG])

Per Dave's research `design/research/short-u-minimal-pair-and-future-vowel-openers.md` §3.1 (verbatim recommendation [STRONG] for `sit / seat`), the **first short-i session opens with an explicit `/i/` vs. `/ɪ/` minimal-pair contrast line**:

> **_"Listen carefully: 'sit' — not 'seat.' Sit! /s/-/ɪ/-/t/. Short 'i' is quick and low."_**

This is **load-bearing first-encounter scaffolding for L1 Tagalog interference + intra-English-confusion.** Two independent pressures stack here:

1. **L1 Tagalog interference (Dave §3.1):** Tagalog has /i/ (high front tense) but English short-i is /ɪ/ (high front lax). Under PAM/SLM-r the two phones are in a Category-Goodness assimilation relationship — Marian will assimilate /ɪ/ to her existing Tagalog /i/ unless an explicit contrast signal is provided at first encounter.
2. **Intra-English `/i/`-`/ɪ/` confusion (`phonics-sequence-marian.md` §Q1):** Even children whose L1 has both /i/ and /ɪ/ commonly confuse the two short vowels in English; the `/ɛ/`-`/ɪ/` pair is the most-confused short vowel pair in English phonics instruction. The `/i/`-`/ɪ/` contrast (long-i vs. short-i) is the second-most-load-bearing.

Marian's diagnostic confirmed `/ɪ/` as her weakest vowel: "i + s failed", "/i/ sound hard for her" (CLAUDE.md + `project_diagnostic_results`). This is **exactly the SLM-r + intra-English combo** Dave's research predicts.

**The contrast pair `sit / seat` is the canonical recommendation (Dave [STRONG], §3.1):**

- "Seat" is NOT in the short-i CVC pool, so the contrast target is unambiguous (no risk of Marian thinking "seat" is one of the words she's about to learn).
- "Sit" is canonical short-i CVC (rejected from the target pool only because it's a verb — picture-chip read instability — but the word itself is perfectly suited for the audio contrast where no chip is involved).
- The contrast anchors `/ɪ/` against the most likely substitution (long /iː/ rather than Tagalog /i/ — for an L1 Tagalog speaker the two substitution paths converge on the same wrong sound).
- The articulation cue **"Short 'i' is quick and low"** captures the lax-quality and the chin-position simultaneously, mirroring the mnemonic in `phonics-sequence-marian.md` §Q1 ("/i/ makes you smile, /e/ drops your chin").

**Where this lands in code:** `WORD_SONG_TRACK_GUIDE` in `api/_planner.ts` gains a short-i branch whose per-problem chatter, when the focus is `cvc-words-short-i` AND it is the first short-i session for this Marian, includes the contrast opener as a baked planner-template constant. **Mechanism reuses the same lifetime-once gate Kevin shipped in PR #174 for the short-u opener** — see Kevin's impl. The "first time across her career" detection mechanism is shared with short-o `box`/`fox` `/ks/` and short-u `/u/`-vs-`/ʌ/` openers.

The artifact that captures the baked line is the canon JSON at `public/canon/word-song/level-1/cvc-words-short-i.json`. AC9b (below) pins this contract.

**Subsequent short-i sessions do NOT replay the contrast.** Per Dave's research §2.1-2.2, the lifetime-once dose is the right cadence — repetition costs working memory without measurable benefit and risks framing `/ɪ/` as a persistent trouble-spot (motivation cost documented in Deci et al. 1999). One well-placed exposure seeds the perceptual category; the 11-word pool across sessions does the consolidation work.

### Why the short-i opener is more critical than the short-u opener

Dave's research §3.1 explicitly flags `/i/`-vs.-`/ɪ/` as **"the highest-stakes first-encounter line in the entire vowel sequence"** — higher than short-u's `/u/`-vs.-`/ʌ/` opener. Two reasons:

1. **The contrast is intra-category, not inter-category.** `/ʌ/` vs. `/u/` are in different vowel-space neighborhoods (mid-central vs. high-back); `/ɪ/` vs. `/i/` are in the same neighborhood (both high-front, differing primarily in lax-vs-tense quality + secondarily in duration). The audio model must be especially clear; the perceptual gradient is finer.
2. **Marian's prior-art confusion is documented.** Short-u was a prediction (Dave §3.1 inferred from the SLM-r mechanism); short-i is a confirmed failure-point in Marian's diagnostic. The opener is doing remediation work on a known-weak skill, not just preempting a predicted weakness.

**Implication for the impl ticket:** the short-i opener line MUST be reviewed against the Azure TTS rendering at canon-bake time. If Emma's voice (rate `-10%`, `en-US-EmmaMultilingualNeural`) renders "sit" with even slight tense quality (closer to "seet" than "siht"), the contrast collapses. Flag for Kevin: TTS A/B at canon-bake; if needed, escalate to Dave for a phoneme-override (`<phoneme alphabet="ipa" ph="sɪt">sit</phoneme>` per `project_audio_phoneme_overrides` precedent for "four" → /fɔːr/).
