# Guidance layer: waiting, suggestions, counters, same-day replay

## Question

Thomas tested the clay Hub after several sessions and could not tell what the stardust (36) and sun (1) meant, or why nothing moved. Quality bar 14 is now confirmed: every screen says what to do next, waiting progress says why and when, and every number has a meaning a child can see. Kyle needs five rules to mock up the guidance layer against the unlock rule (3 good days at 7/8 or better, on separate calendar days, any order, never lost).

## Bottom line

Say the wait out loud, and show it as flower slots that fill up. Emma suggests one world and the other stays open. Remove the day-streak sun. Take the stardust total off the Hub and use stars only inside a session, where they visibly grow today's flower. Same-day replay is allowed and praised as practice. Emma says so honestly: no new flower today.

## Evidence

- **Kidd, Palmeri & Aslin (2013)**, "Rational snacking", _Cognition_ 126(1):109-114. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3730121/ Children waited about 4x longer (12 vs 3 min) after the adult had kept earlier promises. Waiting is a bet on whether the world is reliable, not only self-control. Moderate: a single small study (N=28, age 4-5), but it fits the wider literature. _Use:_ a wait goes down well when its rule is clear and the promise is always kept.
- **Wilhelm et al. (2013)**, _Nature Neuroscience_ 16(4):391-393, doi:10.1038/nn.3343. Children aged 8-11 gained more explicit knowledge from a night's sleep than adults did. Moderate: one study, and a motor-sequence task rather than arithmetic or phonics. _Use:_ "your brain grows while you sleep" is true, not a cover story.
- **Radesky et al. (2022)**, "Prevalence and characteristics of manipulative design in mobile applications used by children", _JAMA Network Open_. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/ They found manipulative design in about 4 of 5 apps used by 3-5-year-olds. The cited examples include **"Come back tomorrow and get a dragon"** and characters nagging idle children. Moderate (cross-sectional coding study). _Use:_ "come back tomorrow" is exactly the line we need, and also a documented dark pattern. The difference is whether it _explains a rule_ or _dangles a prize_.
- **Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999)**, meta-analysis of 128 studies, _Psychological Bulletin_ 125(6):627-668. https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf Expected tangible rewards lowered free-choice intrinsic motivation. That held for completion-contingent rewards (d = -0.36) and performance-contingent ones (d = -0.28). Verbal and informational feedback did not harm it. Strong. _Use:_ stardust is fine as feedback ("you got it"). It turns harmful once it becomes a currency to earn and spend.
- **Patall, Cooper & Robinson (2008)**, meta-analysis of 41 studies, _Psychological Bulletin_ 134(2):270-300. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298272/ Choice raised intrinsic motivation, effort and perceived competence. The effect was stronger for children and with 2-4 options. Strong. _Use:_ two worlds is a good size of choice. Keep both open and add a suggestion; don't take the choice away.
- **Mueller & Dweck (1998)**, _JPSP_ 75(1):33-52. Fifth graders praised for effort persisted more after failure than those praised for being smart. Moderate. Later mindset meta-analyses report smaller effects than the original. _Use:_ praise practice and effort ("you practised"), never "you're so smart".
- **Clinical judgment (no single citation):** most 8-year-olds understand "tomorrow" and "one more day" reliably. "In 2 days" is better shown than said. For an ESL emerging reader, short spoken sentences plus one icon beat any written explanation.

## Application to Marian

- She is Tagalog-first. Every guidance line has to work by ear with no reading: at most about 8 words, high-frequency words only, one idea per line, paired with an icon.
- The unlock rule (3 separate days, never lost) is already child-friendly. Its problem is that it is invisible. The sun streak is a _second, contradictory_ model: it counts consecutive days and can drop to 0. She would be watching a number that resets while the rule that matters never resets.
- Under Kidd's finding, a wait is trusted if it is predictable. So the "tomorrow" promise must always be kept: tomorrow's good session must visibly add the flower.

## Recommendations

**1. Waiting until tomorrow: both spoken and visual, sleep-and-grow framing.**

- Visual: on each Hub card and at session end, show **3 flower slots** for the current step (filled / empty). This replaces any bare number. A slot earned today shows a small moon or sleeping flower until tomorrow.
- Spoken, once, at session end, as information and never as a lure:
  - Flower earned: "You got a flower! It sleeps tonight. Come back tomorrow for one more."
  - Last flower still to come: "One more flower, and a new path opens!"
  - Under 7/8: "Good work! Play again to get today's flower."
- Never use: "locked", "you can't", "wait", countdown timers, push notifications, idle prompts, prizes promised for returning.

**2. Suggest one world: yes, a moderate nudge with a real choice.**

- Highlight one card (gentle glow or bounce). Both cards stay the same size and both stay tappable.
- Emma names the suggestion and gives the choice back: "Let's grow a flower in Number Garden! Or pick Word Song."
- Rule for the suggestion: the world that can still earn today's flower. If both can, pick the one closer to an unlock, then alternate. If neither can, use the practice line in item 4.

**3. Counters: remove the sun, and take the stardust total off the Hub.**

- _Sun (day streak):_ remove it. It contradicts the never-lost unlock rule and puts loss pressure on her (Radesky). The flower slots are now the one progress number.
- _Stardust:_ keep the per-correct star sparkle as in-session feedback, and show the stars collecting into today's flower at the end. Remove the running total (36), which means nothing.
- Do **not** make stardust spendable (shop, unlockables). That would turn informational feedback into an expected tangible reward (Deci et al.) and start a grind loop.
- If Thomas wants to keep a lifetime counter, put it in Parent Settings, not on the Hub.

**4. Playing again the same day after earning today's flower: allow it, praise it, and be honest.**

- Hub, before the session: "Your flower is sleeping. Want to practise more?"
- End of the session: "Great practice! Your brain got stronger."
- Don't block replay and don't nag. If the _other_ world can still earn today's flower, suggest that first, as in item 2.
- No new flower and no bonus, so the rule stays one rule.

**5. Beat order, so she feels carried.**

- **Hub:** (a) Emma greets her and names one next action, with the card highlighted. (b) Each card shows its 3 flower slots. (c) If everything is done today, Emma says so and offers practice.
- **Session end:** (a) praise the effort or the skill ("You read 8 words!"). (b) Animate the flower flying into its slot, then say "2 of 3" out loud. (c) Say what comes next and when ("Come back tomorrow" or "Play Word Song now?").
- **Unlock day:** the new step opening is the climax, and Emma names it.

**For Matt:** this is a copy, Hub-card and session-end change, plus removing one counter. New Emma lines need a canon bake and go through voice-QA (bar 2).

## Risks / counter-evidence

- The line between "come back tomorrow" as information and as a lure is thin and comes from my clinical judgment, not from a study. It is safe only if it is said once, at session end, never by notification or idle prompt, and with no prize promised.
- Removing the streak gives up a possible motivation lever. One 2026 kindergarten study linked app streaks to numeracy gains (cited in `math-distractor-and-streak-decisions.md`). That study did not test streaks that reset, and the flower slots keep the "days count" feeling without loss.
- I have no direct evidence on flower slots versus a progress bar for this age. Both are defensible. Slots match the 3-day rule exactly.
