(A) PICTURES IN THE WORD BOXES (Thomas: "so it's not so hard")

Today (E):

- An empty slot is a cream card: a blank white picture box over three empty boxes (critters-word-hunt.md:74-87; HuntBoard.tsx:330-346).
- Slots are not tied to words. A find fills the next empty slot in find order (critters-word-hunt.md:177; LetterCritters.tsx:1186, :1238), so an empty slot has no word it could show.
- After a find, the slot shows the word's picture, if it has one, over its three tiles (critters-word-hunt.md:182; HuntBoard.tsx:275-293).
- Today a picture shows before a find only through the hint. After 5 runs without a new target, or 45 s idle, the first empty slot glows. From its first tap, the hinted word's picture shows at 35 % (critters-word-hunt.md:208-210; LetterCritters.tsx:998-1006; HuntBoard.tsx:349-361).

Ruling:

1. Each slot belongs to one target, fixed at the deal. Board order is: the picture words with a blend clip a-z, then the other targets with a picture a-z, then the targets without one a-z. The first slot is then always the easiest, and it is the one the hint glows on, so huntHintTarget (critterHunt.ts:262) becomes "the first empty slot's word". A find fills its own slot. The completion lap goes in board order, not find order. R: with fixed slots, find order would jump around the board.
2. An empty slot always shows its word's picture, in full colour, from the deal, over empty boxes. Found means tiles in the boxes, and the picture is the same before and after. I chose "always" over the other three options (R):
   - "Only at first" takes the cue away while she still needs it.
   - "After a miss": a hunt has no misses. Tying help to a non-word would mark it as a mistake, which D5 rules out (play-place.md:836).
   - "As a hint tap" is what build 23 has, and it is what Thomas found hard.
3. A tap on an empty slot plays its word every time, sound only, with no caption, because the caption would be the answer:
   - a picture word plays the word part of its blend clip (wordWindow, as a filled-slot tap does, critters-word-hunt.md:190);
   - any other target plays its critterWordLine clip.
     E: young children name pictures differently from adults, give more alternative names, and fail to name more often (Cycowicz, Friedman, Rothstein & Snodgrass 1997, Journal of Experimental Child Psychology 65(2), 171-237, norms for 5-6-year-olds; I read the abstract only). R: several pack pictures have two names (cab or taxi, cot or bed, nap or sleep, hut or house, jet or plane, tap or faucet). The sound settles which word is meant.
4. A target with no picture shows a drawn listen mark in its picture box ($0), and its tap plays the word. That is a dictation slot, still a sounds-to-letters task.
5. The hint keeps its trigger and its static glow on the first empty slot. Its tap plays the sounds, then the word:
   - a picture word plays its whole blend clip, as today;
   - any other target plays its three critter sounds in order (the critters' shipped letter windows, play-place.md § 3.4), then its word clip.
     The 35 % ghost goes, since the picture is already there. Any new target ends the glow, as today. 0 new audio.
6. The other rules are unchanged: completion at N − 1, the last slot still findable, the extras and their bonus chip. Watch item (Dave (3)): if she shows "I must get them all" distress at the last picture, remove the empty slots' pictures and keep the beat.

What the picture does to the task:

- Without it the hunt is a search: make every word 6 letters allow (Making Words, Cunningham & Cunningham 1992, as in Dave (2)). R: 3 seats from 6 critters give 120 orders before twins. An early reader finds words mostly by trying runs and hearing them sung.
- With it the task becomes: name the picture, say it slowly, find each sound's letter, and seat them in order. That is encoding a known word, the word-box (Elkonin) task. The slot is already a word-box card: a picture over one box per sound. E: word-box instruction has been studied as a first-grade phonics approach to word identification and spelling (Joseph 2000, Reading Research and Instruction; Joseph 2000, Reading Horizons 41(2)). I read only their summaries, not their results, so I claim no effect size.
- R: for her this is the better-matched skill. It runs the path's CVC content in the other direction, from sounds to letters. What is lost is discovery; the extras keep it.

(C) THE RAFT TRIP

Thomas found three crossings too short. My ruling: no crossing cap. A trip ends at its island's party or when she taps Back.

- RAFT_CROSSINGS (playPlace.ts:60) and tripOver (raftModel.ts:310) go.
- raft-island-goal.md § 6, the ordinary trip end, goes.
- Dave raft (4)'s "ends at the third crossing or at the party, whichever comes first" becomes "ends at the party".
- ACs I8 and I16 change (raft-island-goal.md:433, :444).
- The picker, Back and the party are unchanged.

Reasons:

- Cited: Thomas's build-23 observation.
- My rail asks for natural stopping points every few minutes, with no autoplay and no "one more?" (real-games-2026-10-20.md:89). Every crossing already is one: the raft arrives, the scene plays, and the raft comes back empty and waits for ever (play-place.md:307; "A raft or a log waits for ever", play-place.md:175). The forced exit at 3 is not what that rail asks for.
- E: in a 3-week deployment in 24 homes, video content that auto-continued after a preschooler's planned videos extended viewing and reduced children's self-regulation, while a lock-out reduced neither viewing time nor parent intervention (Heung, Hiniker, Hong & Kientz 2018, "Coco's Videos", CHI 2018). I read only the abstract, and it is preschoolers and video, not games. It supports what we keep, which is no autoplay, and suggests a rigid cut-off is not the protective part.
- E (computed, Dave raft (1)): with the shore guarantee, filling the 10 island takes a median of 8 / 6 crossings, 90th percentile 11 / 7, worst 18 / 13. At 3 crossings a trip, the island takes 2-3 re-taps of the tile, and a trip can end one load before the ninth kind.
- R: a crossing takes about 30-70 s (play-place.md:320, plus the walk home, raft-island-goal.md:86). A median of 6-8 crossings is about 3-9 minutes, so most learners reach the party in one sitting, inside my 5-10 minute target (real-games-2026-10-20.md:116, my judgement).
- R: any fixed number (5, 6) still cuts off a learner mid-load or mid-goal for no reason she can see. Thomas's note is about the cut, not the count. Once both islands are full, the raft is a toy, and a toy may have no end (real-games-2026-10-20.md:89; D7 for the free meadow).

Safeguards kept:

- No autoplay; no line invites another crossing (C8); Emma never suggests the raft; no new writes.
- The watch "lessons at least about 2/3 of app time over a week" (real-games-2026-10-20.md:119) matters more now. If the raft displaces lessons, the documented lever is the Parent Settings play limit, off by default and unbuilt. Thomas chose "No parent play limit for now" (play-place.md:9), so that is his call if it shows.

Vehicle: ship this with the queued island-goal build, not before it.

- E: today's island has no homes. Its spots wrap by row (islandSpot, playGeometry.ts:225-231), so with no cap the animals stack on earlier ones after about 8-10 (Dave raft (1): 10 cells on a phone in portrait, 8 in landscape).
