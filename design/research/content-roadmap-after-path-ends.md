# Content roadmap after the path ends

**Date:** 2026-10-15. **For:** Matt, relayed to Thomas. **Status:** research only. Nothing gets built from this until Thomas says go.

## Question

Both paths currently end here: Word Song at `simple-sentences` ("reading sentences") and Number Garden at `mult-6-9` ("big groups") (`packages/core/src/sessionEnd/friendlyNodeName.ts`, `STAGE_SPOKEN_NAMES`). Marian found her first sessions (2026-10-14) too easy in both worlds. In maths she was mostly right but counted on her fingers. With the fast track she may reach the ends sooner than planned. What should come next in each world, what does each step need, and should we extend the paths or deepen the existing steps first?

## Bottom line

- **Maths: deepen first, then extend.** Missing content isn't her gap, because the path already ends in grade-3 work (×6–9). Her gap is fluency: she gets facts right by counting. The fast track gates on accuracy only, so she can finish the path still counting. Start by adding missing-number problems to the adding steps. Then extend in this order: **times mix → sharing → hundreds → adding hundreds**.
- **Word Song: extend now.** Decoding is her strength, and the next phonics steps reuse today's picture-chip format. Every new picture word is also new vocabulary, which is her real bottleneck. Order: **duck words (ck, ng) → frog words (start blends) → jump words (end blends) → cake words (magic e) → little stories**.
- **Leave out for now:**
  - Fractions: the Danish curriculum puts them in the goal after grade 6.
  - Clock time: Danish "halv tre" means 2:30, which clashes with English "half past two".
  - Vowel teams and r-controlled vowels: they come after magic e.

## Evidence

**Reading**

- **R1. Gough & Tunmer (1986)**, "Decoding, reading, and reading disability", _Remedial and Special Education_ 7:6–10. https://readingrockets.org/resources/resource-library/decoding-reading-and-reading-disability The Simple View of Reading: comprehension = decoding × language comprehension. Strong (foundational model, widely replicated). _Use:_ her decoding is ahead, so language (vocabulary) is what limits her.
- **R2. Rydland, Aukrust & Fulland (2012)**, _Reading and Writing_ 25, doi:10.1007/s11145-010-9279-2. Sample: 67 fifth graders in Norway with Urdu or Turkish as L1. Decoding and L2 vocabulary explained most of the variance in passage comprehension. Moderate: one observational study, and I saw only the abstract (via search). _Use:_ for an L2 child in a Scandinavian school, vocabulary has to grow alongside decoding.
- **R3. UFLI Foundations sequence.** Digraphs (lessons 42–46), then blends VCC/CVCC/CCVC (47–53), then VCe (54–62), then r-controlled (77–83), then vowel teams (84–88). Source: a third-party guide, https://spellingtestbuddy.com/resources/ufli-scope-and-sequence/. UFLI's own placement test (https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/UFLIAssessment_081025_v2.pdf) matches this order according to a search summary, but I could not open the PDF. The order is moderate evidence (an RCT-supported programme); the lesson numbers are weak.
- **R4. Bruck & Treiman (1990)**, _J Exp Child Psychol_ 50:156–178. Grade 1–2 children often leave out the second consonant of a starting cluster (frog → "fog"). Moderate. A 2012 _LSHSS_ study (https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/0161-1461(2012/11-0005)) found that kindergartners represented end blends less often than start blends. Moderate; I saw only the summary. _Use:_ teach start blends before end blends, and use the word with the dropped consonant as the best wrong chip.
- **R5. Tagalog phonology** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_phonology) and **Filipino orthography** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_orthography). /ŋ/ is a Tagalog sound and is written ⟨ng⟩. Loanwords starting with s + consonant are written with a leading _i_ (isports, istasyon). Informational, not graded. _Use:_ "ng" is a free win from Tagalog. "stop" may come out as "istop", which is expected and harmless in a tap task.

**Maths**

- **M1. Common Core.**
  - 2.OA.B.2: "Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies. By end of Grade 2, know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers."
  - 3.OA.A.2 and 3.OA.B.6: division as equal shares, and as an unknown-factor problem.
  - 3.OA.C.7: fluent multiplication and division within 100.
  - Sources: https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/OA/ and https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/OA/. These are standards, not evidence of effect. _Use:_ fluency within 20 is the entry ticket to grade 3.
- **M2. Danish Fælles Mål, matematik, after grade 3.**
  - Binding competence goal: develop methods for calculating with natural numbers.
  - Binding attention points: use three-digit numbers to describe amount and order; add and subtract simple natural numbers mentally.
  - Advisory skill goals: methods for multiplication and division; base-ten structure.
  - Rational numbers first appear in the goal after grade 6.
  - Source: https://www.uvm.dk/-/media/filer/uvm/udd/folke/pdf24/maj/240513-faellesmaal-matematik.pdf. I only have the wording from search excerpts and could not open the PDF, so check it before quoting it to the school. _Use:_ this is the grade-3 bar her school actually works to.
- **M3. Fuchs et al. (2010)**, a 16-week RCT with third graders who have maths difficulties. Strategic counting combined with deliberate practice gave better fact fluency than strategic counting alone (ES 0.61). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2850218/ Moderate: a single RCT with struggling learners. _Use:_ counting is a route to recall, not a fault. Recall comes from counting efficiently plus spaced practice.
- **M4. Taylor & Rohrer (2010)**, _Applied Cognitive Psychology_ 24:837–848, doi:10.1002/acp.1598. A day later, fourth graders who had practised mixed problem types scored about 77%, against 38% for blocked practice. The figures come from the authors' group's summary: https://retrievalpractice.org/archive/2017/interleaving. Moderate: a 2019 meta-analysis found strongly varying results across studies. _Use:_ add one mixed times-facts step after the blocked ×2–×9 steps.
- **M5. Spelke & Tsivkin (2001)**, "Language and number: a bilingual training study", _Cognition_ 78:45–88. https://www.harvardlds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/spelke2001b-1.pdf Bilingual adults retrieved exact arithmetic facts better in the language they were trained in, while approximate number knowledge transferred. Moderate: a small adult sample. _Use:_ facts drilled in English may come slower in Danish class. Keep the written equation on screen, because digits are the same in every language.
- **M6. Klein et al. (2013)**, "Language influences on numerical development: inversion effects on multi-digit number processing", _Frontiers in Psychology_ 4:480. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00480/full Children whose language says the ones before the tens make more digit-order errors when they write numbers, and the review lists Danish as one of those languages. Moderate (a review). _Use:_ make the swapped number (342 → 324) one of the wrong chips in the hundreds steps.
- **M7. Siegler et al. (2012)**, "Early predictors of high school mathematics achievement", _Psychological Science_, doi:10.1177/0956797612440101. Knowledge of fractions and division at ages 10–12 predicted algebra and overall maths at 16, after controls. Strong (two national datasets), but only indirect for an 8-year-old. _Use:_ supports a division step. Fractions can wait for the Danish timetable.

## Application to Marian

- **Literacy:** decoding is ahead and vocabulary is behind. The April diagnostic showed her decoding "sun" without knowing the word. That makes R1 and R2 her profile: grow words with every step. The phonics steps below use only picturable, useful words, so each step also adds 8–12 words of vocabulary.
- **Maths, fresh data:** the only new data is one session (2026-10-14): mostly right, counting on fingers. A May note said she no longer used her fingers on sums to 10, so we don't know which sums she counts now. I read her as accurate but not yet fluent (my judgment).
- **Maths, Danish school:** she has been in Danish school since August 2026, and maths class there is taught in Danish. The app's maths should line up with the Danish grade-3 bar (M2), not only with the US one (M1). English is taught as a school subject from grade 1 since the 2014 reform (https://www.thelocal.dk/20140811/denmarks-public-schoolchildren-enter-a-new-era, via search summary). Word Song therefore supports a subject she already has.
- **"halv tre":** in Danish it means 2:30 ("half to three"). English "half past two" is the same time but names the other hour. That is why English clock lessons now would fight her Danish ones. This is my own knowledge of Danish, not a cited source.
- **Code limit for three-digit answers:** `packages/core/src/math/extendedProblems.ts:42-50` sends any answer above 99 to the fallback. The reason is that the progress guard accepts recorded answers of 0..99 only, and one recorded 100 makes the whole progress doc load as a fresh start. Any step with three-digit answers must widen that guard first; otherwise she risks losing her saved progress.

- **Why this order:**
  - It follows R3: digraphs, then blends, then magic e.
  - Duck words go first because both sounds are known and "ng" is Tagalog (R5). It is a two-day step that grows the word pool.
  - Start blends come before end blends (R4).
  - Magic e brings in a new idea: a letter that makes no sound of its own. Her Tagalog reading has taught her that every letter sounds (`phonics-sequence-marian.md`, section "Tagalog orthographic advantage"), so Emma must say this out loud in her intro.
  - Little stories come last because it is the only new interaction, and it reads better once about 40 more words exist.
- **Little stories:** the scene picture appears _after_ she answers, never before. Otherwise the picture answers the question (the decode-first protocol in `phonics-sequence-marian.md`, Q6). The scenes are nice to have, as they were for simple sentences.
- **Next after these:** vowel teams (ee, ai/ay, oa) and r-controlled vowels. _sheep_ and _shark_ are already hybrid sight words, so those steps will feel familiar.

### 2. Number Garden: deepen now, then four steps (after "big groups")

**Deepen inside the existing steps, before she reaches ×6–9:**

- **Missing-number problems in add-to-10 and add-to-20.** Example: "Seven and what make ten?" with `7 + ? = 10` on screen and the same three chips.
  - This builds bonds to 10, the top drill from the April diagnostic.
  - It is the same shape as division later ("how many 3s make 12?").
  - It needs a new read-line template and parser support, but no new visual.
- **Keep add and subtract facts in the daily warm-up (#566) until Thomas no longer sees fingers on sums to 10.**
  - Fingers are a scaffold to fade, not a fault to forbid (M3).
  - No timer or speed signal: `speed-feedback-automaticity-marian.md` stands.

### 3. Extend or deepen: which first

- **The rule:** work on whatever limits her now (my judgment, built on R1, M1 and M3).
- **In maths, that is fluency, so deepen first.** If the path were longer, she would only count her way further.
- **In Word Song, that is vocabulary.** The new phonics steps are the cheapest way to add picture-anchored words, so extend now. Don't pad the CVC steps with more words, since she already finds them too easy.

### 4. What should change

- **Matt:** no tickets now. When Thomas says go, run both tracks in parallel.
  - Maths: missing-number deepening → times mix → sharing → guard change → hundreds → adding hundreds.
  - Word Song: duck → frog → jump → cake → little stories.
- **Kyle:** only "little stories" and "sharing" need new interaction specs. The other seven steps are content in existing formats, though each still needs pictures, an audio bake and a voice-QA ear test (bar 2).
- **Thomas:** one check would change this plan. After a "reading sentences" session, ask her "Who sat on the mat?" about a sentence she read. If she reads it but can't say, move little stories up to step 15.

## Risks / counter-evidence

- "Too easy" and "fingers" come from one session. Re-check after a week of play before anything is ticketed.
- Some programmes teach magic e before blends. I put blends first because they add no new letter-sound rule (my judgment, consistent with R3).
- Interleaving effects vary a lot between studies (M4). Times mix is a cheap step even if the gain turns out smaller.
- M5 tested adults. Whether facts trained in English come out quickly in Danish for an 8-year-old is unknown. Keeping digits on screen is a low-cost hedge, not a proven fix.
- The Danish goals here come from search excerpts (M2). Her school's own year plan outranks both curricula: if it teaches clock time or fractions in grade 3, revisit section 2.
- Age-fit check: none of these steps adds a timer, currency, streak or loss mechanic. The fast track's pace guard is unchanged.
